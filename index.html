What you may notice

"When I sleep, my hernia disappears..."

"But 30 mins after I wake up and start working, it comes back."

If you've experienced this, you're not imagining it.

A hernia can become more noticeable when you're standing, coughing, straining or lifting.

And when you lie down and relax, the bulge may become smaller or disappear temporarily.

That's because a hernia is related to tissue pushing through a weakened area of the abdominal wall.

The important thing is this:

A bulge disappearing temporarily does NOT necessarily mean the underlying hernia has disappeared.

Some people have little or no pain.
Others experience:

  • Pressure.
  • Burning.
  • Dragging.
  • Aching.
  • Pain while lifting.
  • Discomfort after standing for long periods.

And in some cases, the hernia gradually becomes larger.

Understanding the cause

You didn't "cause" your hernia by yourself.

Here's what can happen inside

The abdominal wall

Your abdominal wall normally acts like a strong protective layer around the structures inside your abdomen.

But when an area becomes weak, abdominal tissue can push through that weak point.

Depending on the type and location, the protruding material may include intestine, fatty tissue or other abdominal contents.

Think of it like pressure pushing against a weakened section of a wall.

The pressure doesn't necessarily create the entire problem by itself.

There can already be a vulnerable area in the abdominal wall.

Then repeated pressure can make the bulge more noticeable.

Factors associated with abdominal/groin hernias include:

Heavy lifting

Repeated heavy lifting can increase pressure inside the abdomen.

Prolonged straining

Straining during bowel movements or urination can increase abdominal pressure.

Chronic coughing

Repeated coughing creates repeated increases in abdominal pressure.

Long periods of standing or walking

For some people, prolonged periods on their feet can make symptoms more noticeable.

Constipation

Repeated straining to pass stool can increase pressure on the abdominal wall.

Excess body weight

Additional abdominal pressure can contribute to the strain placed on the abdominal wall.

Age and connective-tissue strength

Some people have naturally weaker connective tissue or develop weakening with age.

So if you developed a hernia after years of physical work...

It doesn't mean you did something wrong.

It means there may be several contributing factors involved.
